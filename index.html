How it's done

Spotless glass with pure water

Tap water and spray cleaners leave something behind when they dry. Central Indiana's hard water is full of dissolved minerals, and glass cleaner leaves a thin film of soap. Pure water has nothing left in it, so it dries clear.

How we clean high glass

Pure water reaches every story

Our filtration cart strips the minerals out of tap water. A soft brush on a long pole scrubs the glass and frames, then a pure-water rinse dries clear with no soap film or spots.

Upper-story windows get cleaned from the ground, so there are no ladders leaning on your siding or crushing your landscaping.

Tap water in

STEP 2

Filter

Sediment and carbon filters catch grit, rust and chlorine.

STEP 3

Deionize

Resin in the tall steel tank pulls out the dissolved minerals, leaving water close to zero dissolved solids.

Minerals: near zero
STEP 4

Scrub and rinse

Pure water flows through a soft brush on the pole. It loosens dirt, then rinses glass and frames.

Soap used: none
STEP 5

Dries spot-free

With no minerals or soap in the water, there's nothing left on the glass when it dries.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    How often should I have my windows cleaned?

    Most homes look their best with a cleaning in spring and fall. Homes near construction, busy roads or lots of trees may want it more often.

    Do I need to be home?

    For exterior-only cleanings, usually not, as long as we can reach the windows. For interior work, someone needs to be home to let us in.

    What if it rains after you clean?

    Rain on clean glass dries much clearer than rain on dirty glass, because there's no dust or film for the water to carry around.

    Can you remove hard water stains?

    Light spotting usually comes off in a normal cleaning. Heavy, long-standing mineral stains can need extra treatment, so mention them when you reach out and we'll take a look.
